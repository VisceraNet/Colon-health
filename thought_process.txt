Layer-CAM is the most suitable method for your specific constraints (intermediate layers, spatial preservation, and CNN-Transformer hybrid).
For your specific "post-hoc interrogation" approach where you analyze intermediate CNN feature maps to preserve spatial structure, standard Grad-CAM is mathematically ill-suited.
Primary Recommendation: Layer-CAM
Why it fits your protocol:
Solves the Intermediate Layer Problem: Standard Grad-CAM calculates a single scalar weight (via Global Average Pooling) for each feature map. When applied to intermediate layers (which have high spatial resolution), this destroys the fine-grained spatial importance of the gradients.
Mechanism: Layer-CAM computes pixel-wise weights (using element-wise multiplication of gradients and activations). This allows it to highlight specific spatial locations within a single feature map, rather than weighting the whole map uniformly.
Result: It produces reliable, high-resolution heatmaps even from earlier convolutional layers, exactly matching your requirement to "preserve spatial structure" before the transformer aggregation. 
Secondary Recommendation: Score-CAM
Why it fits:
Gradient-Free Stability: If you find your gradients are noisy (common in medical models trained with complex losses), Score-CAM is a robust alternative. It masks the input with the feature map and checks the forward-pass score.
Trade-off: It is computationally expensive. If your intermediate layer has 512 channels, Score-CAM requires 512 forward passes per image. Since you are doing this post-hoc (not during training), this latency is likely acceptable for the gain in faithfulness. 
Why NOT Standard Grad-CAM?
Loss of Detail: By averaging gradients, Grad-CAM "smears" the importance. In WCE images, where a lesion might be a small texture change, Grad-CAM on an intermediate layer often produces a coarse blob that doesn't align with the actual mucosal pattern you are trying to study. 
Implementation Strategy for Your "Binary vs. Ordinal" Setup
Since you have two heads, you must generate two separate sets of maps for the same input image to inspect the divergence.
For the Binary Head (Active vs. Remission):
Target: The logit for the "Active" class.
Method: Layer-CAM on the final CNN block (just before the Transformer fusion).
Goal: Verify if the model focuses on the lesion itself or irrelevant bubbles/debris (common WCE artifacts).
For the Ordinal Head (Mayo 0-3):
Target: The logit for the predicted severity class (e.g., Mayo 2).
Goal: Compare the Mayo 2 map vs. the Mayo 3 map.
Insight: If the attention covers a larger surface area for Mayo 3 than Mayo 2, it confirms your model essentially learned "extent of disease" as a proxy for severity (a common clinically valid heuristic).
Summary: Switch to Layer-CAM to properly utilize the spatial resolution of your intermediate layers. Use Score-CAM as a validator if the gradient-based maps look noisy.
